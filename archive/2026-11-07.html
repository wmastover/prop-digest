Rightmove has nudged its revenue forecast down, house prices fell at the fastest pace in over three years, and MJ Gleeson is braced for a slow spring. Coffee up, let's go.

The big one

Rightmove trims its revenue forecast as buyer demand and sales slip

Rightmove now expects 2026 revenue growth of 6% to 7%, down from the 6% to 8% it guided to before. Property Industry EYE ties the cut to weaker buyer demand and falling sales, while The Negotiator points to extra AI spending. It also expects to hand more than £400m back to shareholders between July 2026 and July 2027. Why it matters: a portal growing more slowly while agents are feeling the squeeze is exactly the moment to watch, especially with the £1.5bn agent claim still in play. A cheeky reminder that the portal's numbers are the market's numbers too. Property Industry EYE

📊 The numbers

UK house prices fall at steepest rate in more than three years

Lloyds says average prices dropped 0.6% in October to £296,285, the fourth monthly fall in a row. It's the steepest drop since August 2023, when it fell 1.6%. So what: high mortgage rates are doing what high mortgage rates do, and nobody on the sales side will enjoy hearing that. Guardian Property

🌍 Elsewhere

MJ Gleeson sees a weaker first half of 2027

The affordable housebuilder says its open-market net reservation rate rose slightly year on year in the eight weeks to 1 November. It still expects sales to be slow over the next half year. Fewer new homes sold means fewer homes for agents to sell, so that's one to file away. Property Week

Manchester signs up to unlock up to £500m with National Housing Bank

The Greater Manchester Combined Authority has agreed a deal with the National Housing Bank that could unlock up to £500m. It follows last year's Good Growth Fund launch. Big public money, but the article doesn't say what gets built or when. Property Week
