The big one

Bank of England lifts rates to 4%, first rise in three years

The Bank of England has hiked interest rates to 4% to keep rising inflation under control, according to The Negotiator. That's a blow for anyone with a tracker or a remortgage coming up, and it lands on a market that was already wobbling. Today's Negotiator piece also says Andrew Bailey warns inflation is expected to rise to 5% next year, and a rate setter has called for a further 0.5% hike, so the direction of travel isn't over. Why it matters: higher rates cool buyer demand and squeeze affordability, so expect more of the cold feet and price cuts we've been seeing. Note the two articles don't line up neatly on the detail, so check the Bank's own announcement before you quote the numbers. The Negotiator

🏛️ Rules and politics

Bank of England rate setter calls for 0.5% hike

Andrew Bailey is quoted warning that inflation is expected to rise to 5% next year. Why it matters: if that plays out, the rate story isn't over, and anyone thinking rates have peaked should think again. The Negotiator

Court orders Urban Splash to pay £46.5m fire safety bill

A tribunal has ordered the developer to pay £46.5m to remediate fire safety defects in seven Manchester buildings it developed. The article says this ends a near three-year dispute that was brought on by government. Why it matters: it's a clear signal that developers, not just leaseholders, can end up on the hook for remediation. Property Week

📊 The numbers

Savills sees house prices falling 1.5% in 2027

Emily Williams of Savills predicts a 1.5% fall in property prices next year as the housing market keeps struggling. That's a shift from Savills' earlier view that prices won't recover until 2028, and it's a grim one for anyone betting on a bounce. The Negotiator

Where a home costs under two years' earnings

Zoopla's Richard Donnell has revealed where homes cost less than two years of household earnings. The study shows a stark divide between northern and southern markets, according to Property Industry EYE. Worth a look if you're pitching affordability stories this week. Property Industry EYE

🏢 Portals and big agencies

Foxtons blames the Renters' Rights Act for a lettings slump

In the three months to September, Foxtons' lettings fell 7% year on year and overall revenue dropped 6%. Its Q3 update also flagged a rise in tenant terminations since the rental reforms came in. Property Industry EYE puts the Act's cost to Foxtons at £5.7m, with sales revenue down 10%. Why it matters: this is one of the first hard numbers showing what the new rules cost a big agency, and it won't be the last. Property Week

🗣️ Who said what

Vistry's Adam Daniels on closing 12 of 25 offices

Adam Daniels of Vistry called the decision to shut 12 out of 25 offices "difficult but necessary". Vistry is also cutting staff as it halves its operating regions to 12, after a £661.3m pre-tax first-half loss in September. Our take: "difficult but necessary" is what you say when the spreadsheet made the call for you. The Negotiator

Derwent London has completed £290m of disposals toward a £400m capital recycling target. New chief executive Jonathan Murphy says strong leasing has cut EPRA vacancy and that 2026 is on track to be one of the group's best years on record. Property Week
