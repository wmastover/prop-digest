The big one

Price caps on Your First Home will leave many homes ineligible

The price caps on the Your First Home scheme will exclude many properties, according to Andy Morrison of UKPD. If a home sits above the cap, a first-time buyer can't use the scheme to buy it, however much they want it. Why it matters: the scheme only works if the homes it covers are the ones people actually buy. If the caps cut out much of the stock in your patch, expect more frustration when the scheme gets pitched as the answer. The Negotiator

🏛️ Rules and politics

Santander lifts first-time buyer borrowing to six times income

First-time buyers can now apply to borrow up to six times their income with Santander, subject to eligibility. Why it matters: more borrowing power at the bottom end usually means more offers and more pressure on prices, so watch whether the other big lenders follow. The Negotiator

Rent arrears are now the top reason landlords seek possession

The NRLA's Ben Beadle warns that arrears will rise as higher tax on rental income starts to bite next year. Why it matters: this is the squeeze the Renters' Rights Act was always going to test. Landlords under tax pressure have less room to carry a struggling tenant. The Negotiator

🏢 Portals and big agencies

OnTheMarket and Edward Mellor told to fix shared ownership ads

The Advertising Standards Authority has told OnTheMarket and Edward Mellor to correct misleading adverts for shared ownership homes. The ruling is about the ads, not the homes. Why it matters: the ASA is clearly watching how shared ownership gets sold, so check your own listings before someone else does. The Negotiator

🥊 Challengers

Propoly says credit history drives most referencing failures

Letting platform Propoly says credit history plays a significant role in tenant referencing outcomes. It's a platform talking up its own data, so take the framing with a pinch of salt. Why it matters: if credit is the main blocker, agents who explain outcomes early will spend less time defending decisions. The Negotiator

🌍 Elsewhere

Landsec freezes major residential and office development for 18 months

Landsec plans an 18-month freeze on major residential and office developments, cutting committed development exposure by 80% to £200m by March. The firm says "risks on returns on prospective future projects remain elevated". Why it matters: when a FTSE heavyweight stops building, developers are clearly struggling to make the numbers work. Property Week

Planning fee rises could hit SME housebuilders hardest

Planning fees rise in December and again in April, and Newsteer warns this will create further uncertainty. Smaller housebuilders have the least room to absorb the extra cost. Property Week

Almacantar loses cladding cost appeal over Centre Point

A tribunal has dismissed Almacantar's appeal over recovering leaseholder costs at Centre Point. It rejected the argument that the duty to remediate unsafe cladding only covered fire risks. Why it matters: the ruling widens remediation duties, which is bad news for developers and good news for leaseholders footing the bill. Property Week

Anthropic float could pour AI money into London's top end

The Guardian reports that Anthropic's IPO could fuel a rush of tech money into London's high-end property market. Buying agent Jo Eccles says agents once assumed a young tech entrepreneur with a big budget was after a one-bed flat. Why it matters: the prime London agents who adapt to who's actually buying will win the next wave. Guardian Property
