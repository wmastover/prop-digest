The big one

Tribunal reserves judgement as Rightmove legal hearing ends

The hearing on the £1.5bn legal action against Rightmove has finished, and the tribunal is holding its decision. A ruling on whether the claim proceeds, and how, is due in a few months. Property Industry EYE says around 13,000 agents are waiting on it. Why it matters: this is the first real test of whether the portal's position holds up against the agents who pay to be on it. My take: a few months is a long time for the rest of the industry to sit on its hands, so expect plenty of noise in the meantime. The Negotiator

🏛️ Rules and politics

Rent arrears surge 50% in wake of Renters’ Rights Act

Rent arrears are up 50% since the Renters’ Rights Act came in, according to Property Industry EYE. The piece says landlords are facing growing financial exposure. Why it matters: if arrears keep climbing, the cost of the new rules lands on landlords first, and agents managing their books will be the ones taking the calls. Property Industry EYE

📊 The numbers

Rents keep climbing, but warning signs emerge for letting agents

UK rents rose in October, but void periods surged, which Property Industry EYE reads as the rental market starting to slow. Why it matters: rent growth is still positive, but empty homes are where agents' fees disappear. Watch voids more than headline rents from here. Property Industry EYE

Rental market enters its “winter cool down”

Goodlord’s latest Rental Index shows rent growth slowing and voids increasing as the market moves into its quieter period. Why it matters: this lines up with the October figures, so it's not a one-off blip. The Negotiator

🏢 Portals and big agencies

Chestertons reverses sales merger after nine years

Chestertons has reopened its dedicated sales operation, nine years after folding it into the wider business. Why it matters: a rare U-turn in a sector that usually just keeps merging. Whether it works will depend on how much the split costs them. Property Industry EYE

🗣️ Who said what

Nationwide says market allows fixed-rate cuts as trackers rise

Nationwide's Carlo Pileggi says market conditions let the lender cut its fixed rates, even as tracker rates go up. Why it matters: if you're on a tracker, that's your cue to check your deal and ask what you're actually paying. The Negotiator

🥊 Challengers

Former agent launches AI-powered home buying tool

Caspar Harvard-Walls, who has worked at Foxtons and Black Brick, has launched Oskar. He says it helps house hunters find on- and off-market homes that fit their exact needs. Why it matters: off-market listings are where agents have always kept their edge, so a tool that goes hunting for them is worth a sideways look. The Negotiator

💰 PropTech money

Block management proptech raises £21m to disrupt ‘inefficient’ sector

Supa, led by Upad founder Calum Brannan, has raised £21m. It plans to spend the money buying more market share through managing agent acquisitions. Why it matters: it's a roll-up playbook, and it's the same one that's been reshaping lettings agency for years. The Negotiator

🌍 Elsewhere

Viability problems stall work for over 60% of SME housebuilders

A study for the Home Builders Federation finds viability challenges are delaying or halting work for more than 60% of smaller housebuilders. Those firms are also buying fewer new sites and cutting staff. Why it matters: fewer small builders means fewer homes in the pipeline. Property Week
