The big one

Savills says house prices won't recover until 2028

Savills expects average prices to drift lower for the rest of 2026, then fall 1.5% in 2027. Its reasoning is the Middle East war, which keeps mortgage rates higher for longer and makes buyers nervous. Why it matters: this is a forecast, not a fact, but it's a big agency-adjacent voice pushing back on the 'spring bounce' talk. If you're pricing stock or budgeting for a 2027 sale, assume it's a slow grind. Guardian Property

🏛️ Rules and politics

Bank of England may loosen mortgage lending rules

The Bank of England is considering relaxing mortgage lending rules, according to Property Industry EYE. The summary is thin, so the detail is still to come. Why it matters: looser lending rules would widen the buyer pool, but only if the detail is real. Watch the fine print. Property Industry EYE

Rent rises of up to 88% still getting through

Rent increases of up to 88% are still being approved in England, despite the Renters' Rights Act. Tenant challenges are at record levels since the Act came in in May, and both sides say the tribunal system is 'not working for anyone'. Why it matters: the Act was meant to curb unfair rises. If tribunals keep waving big jumps through, the tenant-friendly headlines won't mean much on the ground. Guardian Property

Half of flats sold last year would have qualified for First Homes caps

Connells research says half of flats and two-thirds of new-builds sold last year would have been eligible under the First Homes price caps. Why it matters: if the caps cover most of the new-build stock, the scheme is less a niche policy and more a mainstream one. Developers and first-time buyers will both be paying attention. The Negotiator

Landlords could face a tax bill jump of over £1,000

Rathbones' Charlie Newsome says property owners could see Income Tax bills rise by more than £1,000. Why it matters: with landlord tax changes already stacking up, this adds to the reasons some landlords are heading for the exit. The Negotiator

📊 The numbers

Mortgage rates hit a three-year high, but some lenders are cutting

Borrowing costs have reached a three-year high ahead of the Bank Rate decision, yet major lenders are cutting selected mortgage rates. Why it matters: the headline is bad, but the cuts show lenders fighting for the buyers who are left. Check the product, not the headline. Property Industry EYE

🏢 Portals and big agencies

Foxtons faces shareholder opposition at AGM

Foxtons saw opposition to key votes at its AGM on dividends and share allocation, and has responded. The summary doesn't say how the votes went. Why it matters: this follows the earlier shareholder backlash, so it's a pattern rather than a one-off. The Negotiator

Zoopla claims one in three listed homes was tracked first

Zoopla says one in three homes listed for sale were tracked on the portal before they hit the market, and its homeowner audience has reached 6.4m. This is the portal's own claim. Why it matters: if it's true, Zoopla is getting people to watch before they sell. That's a powerful pitch to agents, whatever the methodology. Property Industry EYE

🌍 Elsewhere

Build-to-rent starts plunge 63%

UK build-to-rent construction starts have fallen 63% in a year, with regional starts down 78%. Why it matters: fewer new rental homes means more pressure on rents in the years ahead. Property Industry EYE
