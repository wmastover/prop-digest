The big one

Your First Home won't supercharge building, says Bellway

The Chancellor has announced Your First Home, a scheme for first-time buyers. Bellway's Jason Honeyman told The Times it will "not supercharge" housebuilding, and The Negotiator's headline calls the boost "modest". Why it matters: if the builders themselves think it's a nudge rather than a shove, don't expect a sudden wave of new homes. Watch the detail, because the scheme only helps if it actually gets more sites moving. The Negotiator

📊 The numbers

Landlords keep £39.75 of every £100 in rent

A study by Let Compliance says mortgage interest takes £49.10 of every £100 of rent, income tax takes £9.94 and compulsory fees take £1.21. Why it matters: that's the sum landlords are weighing up when they decide whether to stay in the game, and it's a grim sum. The Negotiator

🏢 Portals and big agencies

Rightmove's dominance faces a tougher test

Analysts warn that Rightmove's grip on estate agents is coming under growing scrutiny as the market weakens. Why it matters: portals can charge what they like while agents have no choice, but a weak market gives agents a reason to start asking awkward questions. Property Industry EYE

Independent agency buys a rival's lettings portfolio

A long-established estate agency has completed the acquisition of a rival's lettings portfolio. The summary doesn't say much more than that. Why it matters: with lettings under pressure, buying someone else's landlords is a quicker route to growth than building your own from scratch. Property Industry EYE

🗣️ Who said what

Lloyds says the economy is hitting house prices hard

Andrew Asaam of Lloyds says the "challenging economic backdrop" is having a big impact on the property market, against a backdrop of the steepest house price drop in three years. Why it matters: when a lender says the economy is the problem, that's a warning, not a shrug. The Negotiator

LRG says upfront information helps buyers commit

LRG's Neil Louth says upfront information gives buyers greater confidence in the offer they're making. Why it matters: if buyers feel surer of their offers, they're less likely to pull out later, which is the bit agents actually care about. The Negotiator

Virelle says landlords who held on are doing well

Virelle's Emily Robison says landlords who kept their portfolios are seeing stronger tenant demand and potentially better yields. Why it matters: the sellers have left the building, so the ones who stayed are getting the rewards. Take that with a pinch of salt, since it's the view of someone who sells the service. The Negotiator

🌍 Elsewhere

HSPG splits in two as founders go their own way

The housing investment and management company HSPG is demerging, with its activities continuing through two independent businesses. Sevet will be led by Guy Horne and Highdale by David Searle. Why it matters: a founder-led split usually means each half has to prove it can stand alone. Property Week
